# EVERYDAY CHINESE - episode brief format

The user writes each episode brief in this exact format (EP12, 2026-10-27). When asked for the next
episode, copy it, bump the number, pick a topic not in the log below, and change only TITLE, CAST
outfits/roles (her dress: see "She looks gorgeous" below), the set line, SCRIPT and ENDING. Keep every other section word for word (the eye-line line in ACTING AND CAMERA was added after EP12, where the woman sat in profile looking past the man).

Script rules: one intro line to camera, 9-10 dialogue lines alternating MAN/WOMAN, one ending question
to camera; about 85-95 syllables in total (30-40 s at a natural pace); each line ≤ 14 syllables;
pinyin with tone sandhi written as spoken (yí ge, yìqǐ, bú kèqi); Vietnamese natural and short.
No payment, food handling or object exchange in the scene.
Fixed script vs asked length (user rule, 2026-10-29, EP35): when the brief gives EXACT DIALOGUE and a
duration it cannot fill at a natural pace (EP35: ~69 syllables for 40-45 s), keep the exact script at
natural speed and let the video run shorter. Do not ask, add lines or slow anything; say the delivered
length on the preview.
Vocabulary recap (when the brief has one): each word is said at normal speed and followed by a
one-second review pause with its card on screen (SKILL.md, Timing step 5); never three words in a row.
Vocabulary cards while they talk (user rule, 2026-10-29, after EP27; every episode): the user liked
seeing each new word on screen at the moment it is said in the dialogue. Every episode picks 3 key words
(the brief's vocabulary list, or 3 words from the script when it has none) and shows each as a card
(hanzi / tone-marked pinyin / Vietnamese) at its first spoken use in the two-shot, gold-highlighted for
0.6 s on the word, then kept up until shortly before the next card or about 5 s; again for each word
in the recap; cleared before the ending question. One card at a time, upper-left above the heads,
never over a face. Made in post with `scripts/cards.py` (never an older episode's copied overlay script: EP31's EP28 copy drew 150 px hanzi in a gold frame and the user rejected it as too big and cramped) (SKILL.md, Timing step 5), after the captions.

Delivery title (user rule, 2026-10-27): the viewers are Vietnamese, so the final file and its posting
caption use a Vietnamese title, `Tiếng Trung mỗi ngày NN - <topic in Vietnamese>` (EP16: Đi cắt tóc). The caption's first line is
`Tập NN | <short hook>` (EP18: `Tập 18 | Quán hết chỗ? Nói câu này là được ngồi chung bàn ☕`);
see SKILL.md "Caption".
The brief's own header line stays in English for muse.

Viral rules (user rule 2026-10-29, from Chinese-learning Shorts that spread):
- **Hook in the first 1-2 s is the situation, not an intro**: the intro line to camera states the
  everyday problem or the wrong habit (`Đừng nói "Nǐ hǎo" khi vào quán ăn, hãy nói câu này!`,
  "Quán hết chỗ thì nói gì?"), never a greeting, a self-introduction or the episode name. Pick topics
  viewers hit this week: ordering food, asking for Wi-Fi or a charger, haggling, asking the way, a
  seat, the toilet, paying (said, not handed over: no object exchange in the scene).
- **The first second holds the scroll** (user rule 2026-10-30, EP44): the intro line asks the dilemma
  in Chinese and starts within 0.3 s of frame 0 (no silent lead-in), and `scripts/hook.py` burns the
  same dilemma in Vietnamese above her head from frame 0, with the open loop "Cuối video có câu hỏi cho
  bạn!" (SKILL.md Timing step 5, Hook). When the brief fixes a weaker intro ("今天学怎么预约看牙"), keep
  its words and let the Vietnamese hook line carry the question.
- **Hold them past 3 s** (user rule 2026-11-05: "people just watch around 3s and then swipe"; the
  hook faded and the cut to the two-shot landed right at ~3 s with nothing left to wait for):
  - Hook line = pain + promise, ≤ 8 words: the problem, then what one sentence gets them
    ("Sắp lỡ chuyến bay? Hỏi 1 câu là xong", "Đừng nói 你好 khi gọi món!"), never a bare
    topic ("Hỏi đường ở sân bay"). The wrong-habit form ("Đừng nói X, hãy nói…") beats a plain question.
  - The intro is said with urgency (worried, surprised, a little embarrassed), not as a calm
    presenter; she leans in slightly toward the lens. Write the emotion into the ACTING line of the
    intro ("says it with mild panic, eyebrows raised").
  - A second open loop crosses the cut: `REVEAL=<start of the key phrase>` on `scripts/hook.py`
    counts down "Câu trả lời sau Ns" in the top band from the hook's fade until the key phrase,
    then flags it green. So the key phrase (the one the hook promises) must come early, by ~8-12 s,
    in the WOMAN's first or second line, not at the end; the ending quiz then asks it back.
  - No dead air at the cut: the first two-shot line starts within 0.2 s of the cut and is the
    turn of the story (her problem said to the other person), never "你好" small talk.
  - A new thing on screen every 2-3 s through the first 10 s (hook → cut → countdown → first card →
    green flag): put the first vocab card on the first two-shot line, not later.
- **She looks gorgeous** (user rule 2026-10-30, after EP44's plain lavender blouse: "the girl wear beautiful
  dress", "like a video in a party", so viewers keep watching): the woman always wears a beautiful,
  eye-catching dress, never a plain blouse or cardigan, with styled hair (soft waves or an elegant
  updo), natural glam makeup and delicate earrings. Name it fully in CAST: colour, fabric, cut, e.g.
  "emerald satin midi dress with a wrap bodice", "blush-pink chiffon dress with fluttery sleeves",
  "champagne sequined cocktail dress", "red qipao-style silk dress with a mandarin collar". Tasteful and
  fitted, no deep neckline or very short hem. Pick a colour that stands out from the set and from the
  man's clothes. The man is smart to match (blazer, suit, crisp shirt). On an everyday set (clinic,
  shop) the dress is a day dress dressed up (satin, wrap, floral chiffon); intro framing from the knees
  up shows it, the two-shot from the waist up.
- **Party episodes** (about one in three): set the scene at an event where a gown fits: a birthday
  party, wedding reception, rooftop cocktail evening, company year-end party (年会), Lunar New Year or
  Mid-Autumn party, a gallery opening. The topic stays an everyday phrase people need there
  (introducing yourself, complimenting an outfit, asking someone to dance, saying goodbye to the host,
  asking where the restroom is). Set line: "a quiet corner of the party venue, warm string lights and
  balloons softly blurred behind them, no other guests in frame"; no drinks or plates in hand (no
  object handling, and only the cast in frame, as always).
- **Captions show the tones**: three lines hanzi / pinyin / Vietnamese side by side; `burn_subs.py`
  colours each pinyin syllable by tone (1 red, 2 orange, 3 green, 4 blue, neutral white).
- **The ending loops and asks**: the ENDING question is a mini quiz on the episode's key phrase (a
  blank to fill, or "which one is right?") and its wording echoes the hook, so a replay lands back on
  the problem it answers. The posting caption's CTA asks for the answer in the comments
  ("Comment câu trả lời của bạn bên dưới xem đúng không nhé!").
- **Title and cover name the dilemma**: the `Tập NN | <hook>` line and the first frame state the urgent
  everyday problem ("Quán hết chỗ?", "Điện thoại sắp hết pin?"), not the grammar or the word list.
