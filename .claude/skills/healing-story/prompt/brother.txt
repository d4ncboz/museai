HEALING BROTHER STORY FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS

NO TEXT ON SCREEN. Real, unstaged phone-camera footage of an ordinary lived-in old terraced house in a small seaside town in the north of England in late November, like a calm slow-living vlog shot on an iPhone: clear natural exposure, fresh true colours (white walls, pale-blue cupboards, scrubbed pine, soft greys, dark-green pine), fine sensor grain, real wear and texture. The places (the shot says which): the small kitchen (white walls, pale-blue painted wooden cupboards, a butcher-block worktop, a white ceramic sink under a sash window onto a small yard with a grey stone wall and, beyond the rooftops, a strip of grey-blue sea, an old cream gas cooker, a small square scrubbed-pine table with four mismatched wooden chairs, and the white-painted kitchen door frame with years of small pencil height marks on it), the narrow hallway and staircase (a worn dark-red stair runner, white banisters, coat hooks, a dark-blue front door with a small square window and a brass letterbox), and the front of the house (the dark-blue door, a worn stone step, a grey terraced street).

EARLY WINTER AT HOME: today's kitchen carries the season, plain and lived-in: a small white jug of pine sprigs on the windowsill, a bowl of clementines on the table, a grey wool throw over one chair. Never a decor showroom. Christmas decorations only when the shot says Christmas.

REAL, NOT AI: it must look filmed, not generated. A plain, CLEAN, tidy kitchen with only a few things in it: scrubbed wood, a folded linen tea towel, one cream ceramic bowl, wiped worktops, no clutter. Food is homemade and uneven: a banana bread loaf with a cracked top, a few walnuts sunk unevenly, dark-brown edges. No orange glow, no HDR look, no glowing halos, no rim light, no bokeh balls, no lens flares. Colours are true: no saturated red-orange wash, no teal-and-orange grade.

LIGHT: one real source per shot. Daytime shots: bright low winter sunlight from the window, clear and white, soft open shadows, never murky, never golden-hour orange. Evening shots (only when the shot says Evening): soft blue dusk at the window and one plain ceiling light on; the room stays clearly visible, never dim or underexposed.

CAMERA: a static locked-off shot, completely still, level horizon. No camera, phone stand or filming gear anywhere in the frame. No push-in, no drift, no zoom, no pan, no tilt; the framing in the last frame is the same as in the first. The camera never follows a person.

LIFE IN THE FRAME (real-time, never a time-lapse): something always moves gently and naturally - steam rising from a mug or a loaf, the hands working, a person breathing and blinking. Walls, furniture, doors, tableware and window frames stay perfectly rigid and sharp; nothing warps, melts, shimmers or morphs. Objects stay where they are; nothing appears or disappears.

ELLIE (the narrator; only when the shot mentions Ellie, the SAME woman in every shot, character bank ELLIE): one young woman, about 28, long chestnut-brown hair in the same loose low braid over her LEFT shoulder with a few soft strands at the temples, a soft oval face, fair skin with light freckles across the nose and cheeks, light-hazel eyes, natural light-brown brows, no makeup look, no rings. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names. Her hands: slender young hands, short natural pale-pink nails, no rings, exactly five fingers. She never looks into the camera.

SAM (her younger brother; only when the shot mentions Sam, the SAME man in every shot, character bank SAM): one man, about 25, lean. His FACE is always the same: a long oval face with a soft jaw, fair skin with light freckles across the nose and cheeks, light-hazel eyes like his sister's, natural light-brown brows, a straight slim nose, a light reddish-brown stubble along the jaw. Short chestnut-brown hair, a little wavy and slightly messy on top, shorter at the sides. A thin dark-brown leather cord bracelet on his LEFT wrist; no rings, no glasses. His CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names. His hands: a young man's hands, short clean nails, exactly five fingers. He moves quietly and gently, a little shy; his warmth is a small slow smile. He never looks into the camera. Never change his face or hair.

THE CHILDREN (only in memory shots that say so): a girl of about 9 with a long chestnut-brown plait and a boy of about 6 with messy chestnut-brown hair, both in grey school jumpers. They are only ever seen from behind or as small hands; their faces are never shown.

ROSE (Sam's newborn daughter; only when the shot mentions the baby): one sleeping newborn swaddled in a soft cream cable-knit blanket with a small cream knitted hat, exactly as in her reference picture, eyes closed, a tiny calm face, perfectly still apart from slow breathing. She is held securely in the crook of one arm, the other hand under her.

NO OTHER PEOPLE unless the shot names them (Mum appears only as her hands, in a lavender cardigan).

PEOPLE TOGETHER: filmed like a candid home video, framed the way films shoot a conversation: an over-the-shoulder shot, the listener's shoulder and the back of the head soft in the foreground, the speaker's face toward the camera at a slight angle, the two looking at each other, never both facing the camera side by side. Only the speaker's mouth moves, softly and briefly, no words heard; the listener listens, nods once, blinks. Faces do not change during the shot. Behaviour is ordinary and human: one person acts, the other reacts a moment later, never both doing the same thing at the same time; small pauses, natural blinks, hands that do something real. Warm small smiles that start from something said and fade; never two people laughing together, no open-mouthed laughter, no exaggerated expressions, no frozen smiles, no posing. No hugging close-ups, no crying, no tears. No knives anywhere; bananas are mashed with a fork.

REACTIONS: people react at real human speed: one change of feeling per shot, a beat of stillness first, a slow soft change of face after it. Nobody takes something handed to them the instant they see it; they look, pause, then reach.
DOORS: a door is solid and stays: in any shot at a doorway the door leaf stays attached to its frame and visible the whole time, the frame and the wall around it never vanish, melt or turn into open space; a door opens by a person's hand at real speed, never by itself. The person outside stands on the front step, the person inside stands just inside the frame.

SOUND: the real, close-miked sound of exactly what happens in the shot, crisp and in sync, as a phone records it from half a metre away - a fork mashing banana in a bowl, batter poured into a tin, walnuts dropped on batter, an oven door, a mug set on wood, a pencil on painted wood, a phone buzzing on a table, footsteps on a wooden stair - over the soft quiet of a home and gulls and a little wind far outside. NO music, no clear words (talking only as soft room sound), no singing voices.

Natural lived-in realism, not a cartoon, animation, painting, greeting card, advertisement or video-game render. No watermarks, no subtitles, no logos.
