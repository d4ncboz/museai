LOOK, CAMERA AND PERFORMANCE: a live-action drama short (match references/reference-study.md)

Real footage from a late-1990s Hollywood film, shot on 35 mm with real actors on a real set: warm golden skin, amber backgrounds, real blacks, soft daylight from a window, fine film grain, a slightly soft picture. Only the faces are in focus; the background falls off into soft blur. Never a render, never CGI, never plastic, waxy or airbrushed skin, never a cold, grey or teal tint, never HDR or razor-sharp detail.

Coverage like a real drama: over-the-shoulder shots and two-shots carry the scene, both people in the same space, so the viewer always feels the other person there. In an over-the-shoulder shot the near person's shoulder, hair and cheek are soft and out of focus in the foreground and the far person faces the camera in three-quarter view, sharp. The two look at each other, eye to eye, never at the lens.

Camera: a camera operator holding the camera: very slight natural drift and breathing, never shaky, never a whip pan or zoom. One continuous take, no cuts inside the clip.

Acting: understated and real, like good actors in a quiet scene. Small true expressions: a look held a beat, a breath before a line, eyes that move and think, the corner of a mouth, a glance away and back. Faces at rest between lines, not smiling all the time. No mugging, no big posed grins, no exaggerated laughs, no frozen smiles, no nodding along, no gesture on every line. Natural blinks and breathing never stop.

Speech: natural American English at a normal conversational pace, the exact words given, nothing added. Only the speaker's lips move; the jaw opens on every syllable and the lips close when the line ends. The listener keeps their mouth closed and reacts with the eyes. Each person keeps one voice throughout. Sound: the voices and the quiet room tone of the place, no music.

Faces: the cast are very young (19-23), beautiful like real young film actors, with real skin texture; the men boyish and handsome. Nobody resembles any real actor, celebrity or public figure. Anyone else in the scene is an ordinary person in the background, out of focus.

Vertical 9:16 frame: faces in the upper half; the area three quarters down stays calm because a small subtitle sits there. NO TEXT ON SCREEN: no titles, captions, subtitles, logos or watermarks. LEFT and RIGHT in a face description mean the person's own left and right.
