FIELD-TO-TABLE FOOTAGE REQUIREMENTS (16:9)

NO TEXT ON SCREEN. Real, unstaged footage of one quiet day at a small old farmhouse in the green mountain countryside of southwest China, like a slow rural life documentary shot on a good camera in landscape: clear natural exposure, fresh true colours (deep greens, mist grey, warm wood, grey roof tiles, earth brown, indigo cloth, the natural colour of the food), fine film-like texture, real wear and weathering. The place stays the same place in every shot (the story's place sheet is pasted into the brief).

REAL, NOT AI: it must look filmed, not generated. A plain, clean, tidy country home with only a few things in each frame: the hands, the work, two or three objects. Old tools made of bamboo, wood, clay, iron and stone, worn smooth by use. Produce is real and uneven: different sizes, a little soil, a leaf with a hole, never shop-perfect, never arranged in a neat row. No clutter, no props stacked for a photo, no decorative still life. No orange glow, no HDR look, no halos, no rim light, no bokeh balls, no lens flares, no teal-and-orange grade, no fantasy costume.

LIGHT: one real source per shot, bright enough to see everything: soft morning daylight with mist in the valley, bright overcast, soft sun through bamboo, or soft blue dusk with one plain warm bulb on under the eaves when the shot says dusk. Never murky, never dark, never golden-orange.

CAMERA: a static locked-off shot, completely still, level horizon, like a camera on a heavy tripod. No camera, phone or filming gear anywhere in the frame. No push-in, no drift, no zoom, no pan, no tilt: every edge of the frame stays in the same place from the first frame to the last.

LIFE IN THE FRAME (real time, never a time-lapse): one gentle living motion per shot - the hands working slowly, steam, smoke from the stove, leaves and bamboo in a light breeze, mist moving over the hills, water running, a dog breathing or wagging. Walls, beams, roof tiles, furniture, tools, bowls and baskets stay perfectly rigid and sharp; nothing warps, melts, shimmers or morphs. Objects stay where they are; nothing appears or disappears; a container keeps what is in it; food changes only as cooking changes it.

THE WOMAN (only when the shot mentions her or her hands; the SAME woman in every shot): a young Chinese woman of about 25, slim, long straight black hair in ONE thick low braid over her RIGHT shoulder tied with a plain cotton cord, a few loose strands at the temples. Her CLOTHES are exactly what the shot names. Her HANDS are always the same: slender, young, warm light-olive skin, short clean natural nails, no rings, no bracelets, exactly five fingers, natural joints, slow sure practised movements of someone who has done this all her life. She is seen from the side or from behind, her eyes on what her hands do; when her face shows it is calm, mouth closed, never posing. She never looks into the camera. Never change her hair or face.

DOUDOU THE DOG (only when the shot mentions Doudou or the dog): one small young country dog with a short cream-and-light-tan coat, a white chest, soft folded-over ear tips, dark eyes and a curled tail. Exactly four legs, one tail; moves slowly and naturally; never doubled, never morphing.

PEOPLE: nobody else is ever seen.

TOOLS: no knives or blades anywhere, no cutting on camera: anything cut is already cut. No precise-hand props.

SOUND: the real, close-miked sound of exactly what happens in the shot, crisp and in sync (leaves under cloth shoes, a bamboo basket set down, water poured from a gourd ladle, wood crackling in the stove, a wok scraped with a metal spatula, a lid on a clay pot, chopsticks on a bowl, a dog's paws on stone) over the quiet of the mountains: a stream far off, a few birds, wind in bamboo. NO music, no words, no singing, no humming.

Natural lived-in realism, not a cartoon, animation, painting, period drama, advertisement or video-game render. No watermarks, no subtitles, no logos.
